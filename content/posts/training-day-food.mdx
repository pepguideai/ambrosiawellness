There's a version of training-day eating that involves apps, scales, and a lot of arithmetic. Some people enjoy that, and that's fine. This post is for everyone else.

What follows isn't a meal plan. It's a rough shape for the day that we find easy to stick to, and easy to adjust.

## Before you train

If you're training within an hour or two of waking, a small snack is usually enough: toast, a banana, yogurt, whatever sits well. If you train later in the day, a normal lunch a few hours before tends to be plenty.

The main thing is not to arrive starving or uncomfortably full. You'll learn which one you lean toward pretty quickly.

## After you train

We aim for a proper meal within a few hours, built from three parts:

- **Something with protein.** Eggs, beans, fish, chicken, tofu, lentils, Greek yogurt.
- **Something starchy.** Rice, potatoes, pasta, bread, oats.
- **Something with colour.** Whatever vegetables or fruit are in the fridge and need using up.

That's it. A bowl of rice with fried eggs and frozen peas counts. So does a jacket potato with beans.

## Drinking enough

Keep a glass or bottle somewhere you'll see it. Drink when you're thirsty, and a bit more if it's hot or you've been sweating a lot.

## What we skip

We don't track every gram, and we don't buy special training-day products. If you have specific goals, a medical condition, or a history of a difficult relationship with food, a registered dietitian can give you advice that fits you. This is just how we keep things simple.
