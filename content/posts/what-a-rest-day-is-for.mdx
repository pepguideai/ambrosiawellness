If you're new to regular exercise, rest days can feel like falling behind. If you've been at it a while, they can feel like something you've earned. Neither is quite right.

A rest day is just part of the plan. It's the day you let the work from the rest of the week settle.

## Rest doesn't have to mean doing nothing

Some people like a true day off. Others feel better moving gently. Both are fine. A few options for a lighter day:

- A walk, at a pace where you could hold a conversation
- Some easy stretching or mobility work
- A bike ride to run errands
- Playing with kids, gardening, or a long tidy-up

The test is simple: you should finish feeling better than when you started, not tired.

## Use it for the other stuff

Rest days are a good time to do the things that support training but don't feel like training. Cook something for the week ahead. Get to bed a bit earlier. Wash your kit.

## How often

There isn't one right answer. It depends on what you're doing, how you're sleeping, and what else is going on in your life. A sensible starting point is to make sure you have at least one or two easier days each week, and to add more if you're consistently feeling run down.

## A note on guilt

If skipping a session makes you anxious, try putting rest days on the calendar in advance. A planned rest day isn't a missed workout. It's the plan working.
