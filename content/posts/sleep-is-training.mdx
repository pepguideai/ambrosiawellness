When the week fills up, most of us don't cancel the workout. We cancel the sleep. We stay up to finish the thing, or catch up on the show, or get a few minutes that belong only to us.

That's understandable. It's also usually the first thing we'd tell a friend to fix.

## Start with the wake-up time

It's often easier to control when you get up than when you fall asleep. Picking a wake-up time and keeping it most days, weekends included, gives the rest of your routine something to hang on.

## Give the evening an off-ramp

You can't go from answering emails to asleep in five minutes. Try giving yourself twenty or thirty minutes where the day winds down:

- Dim the lights a bit.
- Put the phone on charge somewhere that isn't the bed.
- Do something boring and pleasant: read, stretch, tidy the kitchen.

None of these are magic. They're signals that the day is over.

## Make the room easy to sleep in

A cool, dark, quiet room is a good default. Blackout curtains, an eye mask, or earplugs are cheap experiments if light or noise wakes you.

## Be kind about the bad nights

Everyone has nights where sleep doesn't come. One rough night isn't a failed plan, and lying awake worrying about it rarely helps. Get up, do something quiet, and try again later.

## When to ask for help

If you're regularly exhausted despite giving yourself time to sleep, or you snore heavily, wake gasping, or feel sleepy while driving, talk to a doctor. Those are worth a proper conversation, not a blog post.
